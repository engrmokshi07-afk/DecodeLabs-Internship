## Scenario

A fitness startup wants to launch an app for busy office professionals who struggle to find time for exercise. Before any screen is designed, the team needs to understand the user's pain. This project defines the target audience, creates user personas, and maps what each persona sees, thinks, feels and does.

## Target audience

Office professionals aged 25 to 45 who spend 8 to 10 hours a day at a desk, commute daily, and want to stay active but struggle with time, energy and consistency.

## Key insights

1. **Time, not motivation, is the main barrier.** Users want to be fit but cannot find a reliable block of time.
2. **Past app failures create skepticism.** Generic plans and streak pressure lead to quitting within weeks.
3. **Small wins build the habit.** Short, flexible sessions feel doable and show quick progress.
4. **Guilt and invisible progress drive drop-off.** Missed sessions feel like personal failure, and users stop when they cannot see results.

## Design opportunities for the next phase

- Sessions of around ten minutes that fit into any gap in the day
- Flexible plans that forgive missed days, with no-shame resets
- Honest, personalized starting points instead of generic plans
- Simple milestones and visible progress tracking

## Method

I gathered informal feedback by talking to family and friends about their exercise habits. I asked what they thought about staying fit and what problems they faced in going to the gym. Their answers shaped the personas, the empathy map content and the key insights.

This was informal, small-scale research, not a formal study. The personas are fictional composites based on these conversations.
